# Example Simulation: SecureFlow SaaS (Condensed)

**Company**: SecureFlow
**Mission**: Zero-trust data sharing for regulated industries
**Starting capital**: $750,000
**Team**: 1 founder/CEO, 1 CTO, 2 engineers, 1 CMO
**Objective**: Reach $100K ARR within 8 months
**Market**: Regulated B2B SaaS

## Days 1–5: Foundation

**Day 1**: Founder Avery defines security-first architecture. CTO Blair selects tech stack (React + Python + PostgreSQL + SOC 2 compliance framework). Engineers start auth system. CMO Dana defines target personas (healthcare IT, fintech CTOs). Cash burn: $694/day. Runway: ~1,080 days.

**Day 2**: Competitor "DataShield" announces SOC 2 certification. Competition +0.05. Team accelerates compliance work. Revenue ops tool evaluated for sales pipeline.

**Day 3**: Auth system 60% complete. CMO launches compliance-focused blog series. Early SEO traction. Brand strength +0.03.

**Day 4**: First engineering audit identifies technical debt risk from rapid auth implementation. Casey schedules refactoring. Product progress: 15%.

**Day 5**: Auth complete. CMO's blog post gets 500 unique visitors. 3 healthcare IT leads generated. First paying pilot opportunity identified (potential $15K ARR).

## Days 6–10: Validation

**Day 6**: DataShield launches with SOC 2 certification (competitive threat). Sentiment -0.03. SecureFlow pivots positioning to "better UX + faster implementation." CMO shifts messaging.

**Day 7**: First pilot customer signed ($5K/month for 3 months). Revenue: $5,000. MRR: $5,000. Cash burn still dominates. Runway: ~890 days. Product progress: 35%.

**Day 8**: Second pilot ($8K/month) from fintech. MRR: $13,000. Engineers begin feature build for pilot requirements. CMO starts case study content.

**Day 9**: DataShield lowers price by 15%. Competition +0.04. SecureFlow counters with "enterprise-grade + superior UX" positioning. No churn from existing pilots (high switching costs).

**Day 10**: 3rd pilot signed ($12K/month). MRR: $25,000. ARR on track: $25K × 12 = $300K annualized. Product readiness: 55%. Quality: 0.45. Technical debt: 0.08 (from rapid auth build).

## Days 11–20: Scale

**Day 15**: Team hires 2nd CMO (marketing specialist). Hiring cost: $15K. Payroll increases to $950/day. MRR: $42,000 (across 5 paying pilots). ARR projection: $504K. On track for $100K ARR at 4 months.

**Day 20**: Product readiness: 75%. 7 paying customers. MRR: $68,000. ARR: $816K annualized. Runway: ~650 days. Competitor DataShield launches team collaboration feature.

## Outcome (Day 30)

- **MRR**: $110,000
- **Paying customers**: 12
- **ARR**: $1.32M annualized
- **Runway**: ~450 days
- **Product**: 92% readiness, launch stage
- **Market share**: ~8% of target segment
- **Outcome**: Exceeded $100K ARR objective in 30 days (annualized)
- **Key decision**: Pivot to compliance-first positioning after Day 6 competitor threat

## Causal Chain

```
Competitor SOC 2 → Positioning pivot → 3/3 pilots won → $1.32M ARR
```

# Example Simulation: Bloom & Co Consumer (Condensed)

**Company**: Bloom & Co
**Mission**: Sustainable home goods monthly subscription
**Starting capital**: $100,000
**Team**: 1 founder, 1 marketer, 1 operations
**Objective**: 5,000 subscribers in 6 months
**Market**: Eco-conscious consumers

## Days 1–5: Product-Market Fit Discovery

**Day 1**: Founder establishes supplier relationships (3 sustainable home goods suppliers). Marketer creates Instagram + TikTok accounts. Operations sets up shipping logistics (partner with ShipBob). Cash burn: $208/day. Runway: ~480 days.

**Day 3**: First batch of 20 products received. Quality check passes. Marketer launches unboxing video (12K views). 150 email signups. Brand strength +0.05.

**Day 5**: Soft launch to 50 email subscribers. 32 convert to paid ($39/month). MRR: $1,248. 2 returns (quality issues). Retention: Day 1: 32/50 = 64%. Product stage: beta.

## Days 6–15: Growth Experimentation

**Day 8**: Referral program launched (give $10, get $10). Viral coefficient: 0.23. 89 new signups. 57 convert. MRR: $3,456. CAC: $12 (below $39 LTV).

**Day 12**: TikTok video goes viral (250K views). 1,200 new signups. 780 convert. MRR: $34,000. Inventory needs increase. Operations secures additional supplier. Cash burn accelerates to $450/day (inventory prep).

**Day 15**: Month 1 retention: 82%. Net revenue retention: 95%. MRR: $42,000. Subscribers: 1,092. Runway: ~380 days (inventory investments).

## Days 16–30: Scaling Challenges

**Day 18**: Supplier #3 fails quality audit (defective products shipped to 47 customers). Crisis. Founder issues refunds ($1,833). Trust campaign launched. Brand strength -0.08. 12% churn (131 subscribers lost).

**Day 22**: New supplier #4 onboarded ($15K minimum order). Cash burn: $890/day. MRR: $51,000 (after churn). 2nd viral TikTok (150K views). 890 new signups.

**Day 25**: 3rd supplier fails (delayed shipment). Inventory risk. Founder diversifies suppliers (6 total). Cash: $62,000. Runway: ~70 days at current burn. ⚠ Risk: cash exhaustion.

**Day 30**: 3,847 subscribers. MRR: $150,000. Monthly revenue growth: 350% since Day 5. But cash burn is unsustainable ($890/day = $27K/month). Founder decides to raise $200K seed round at $1M pre-money.

## Outcome (Day 30)

- **Subscribers**: 3,847 (on track for 5,000 by Day 45)
- **MRR**: $150,000
- **Cash**: $62,000
- **Runway**: ~70 days
- **Key decision**: Raise seed round (Day 30) — without it, inventory scaling would exhaust cash by Day 35
- **Lesson**: Viral growth requires inventory + cash buffer; supplier diversification is critical

## Causal Chain

```
Viral TikTok → Surge demand → Inventory investment → High burn → Cash crisis
→ Seed fundraising → Sustainable scaling
```
